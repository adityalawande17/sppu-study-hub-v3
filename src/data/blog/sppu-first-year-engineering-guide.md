First Year Engineering (FE) at SPPU is the same for every branch — Computer Engineering, IT, Mechanical, Civil, everyone sits the same exams. This common foundation year shapes how well you handle the specialised years that follow. Here's what to expect and how to approach it.

## The Common Syllabus

Under both the 2019 and 2024 patterns, FE has a unified syllabus across all engineering branches. There are no branch-specific subjects until Second Year.

Typical FE subjects include:

- **Engineering Mathematics I & II** — calculus, linear algebra, differential equations
- **Engineering Physics** — optics, quantum mechanics, solid state
- **Engineering Chemistry** — polymers, corrosion, fuels
- **Basic Electrical Engineering** — circuits, transformers, machines
- **Engineering Mechanics** — statics, dynamics, friction, beams
- **Programming and Problem Solving** — C/C++ basics
- **Engineering Drawing** — projection, sections, isometric drawing
- **Communication Skills / Professional Communication** — written and spoken English

The 2024 FE syllabus PDF is available on the [Syllabus page](/syllabus).

## How FE Is Different from 11th and 12th

This is where most students get caught off guard. JEE/MHT-CET preparation is nothing like engineering university exams.

**What changes:**

- **Content volume:** A single FE subject covers more material than an entire 12th standard subject in the same topic
- **Exam format:** 5 questions with internal options, not MCQs. You need to write detailed answers, not fill circles.
- **Self-study:** There's no coaching class drilling you every day. You manage your own schedule.
- **Attendance:** Missing class is now your problem, not the school's. The 75% rule is real — [read more about attendance rules](/blog/sppu-attendance-rules-75-percent).

## The Subjects Most Students Struggle With

**Engineering Mathematics I:** The jump from 12th standard maths to university maths is steep. Integrals, matrices, and differential equations at this level need consistent practice, not last-minute cramming.

**Engineering Drawing:** Completely new for most students (unless you had a drawing paper in 12th). It requires spatial reasoning and a lot of practice. Don't skip the practicals.

**Engineering Mechanics:** More conceptual than students expect. Free body diagrams and moment calculations need methodical setup.

## How to Study for FE University Exams

**Previous year papers are still your best tool.** Even in FE, the question patterns are consistent. Go through 3–4 years of papers for each subject before the university exam.

**Don't ignore practicals.** FE has several practical/lab components with external marks. These are straightforward to score well in if you show up prepared — don't treat them as formalities.

**Form a study group.** FE is the year where you meet all your future batchmates. Students who build study groups in FE tend to carry those networks through their entire engineering career.

## The First Sessional Shock

Almost everyone scores lower than expected in their first class test (sessional). This is normal — the gap between school exams and university exam format is real. Don't let a bad first sessional derail you. Focus on the final exam, which carries 80 marks.

## Starting on the Right Foot

The habits you build in FE follow you through SE, TE, and BE:

- **Attend regularly from day one.** It's much harder to recover 75% attendance if you start with gaps.
- **Read ahead of lectures.** Even 10 minutes of preview before a lecture makes it easier to follow.
- **Keep your notes organised.** You'll refer back to FE maths concepts in every later year.
