The **75% attendance rule** is one of the most talked-about (and least understood) rules at SPPU. Here's exactly how it works and what happens when you fall short.

## The Basic Rule

SPPU requires a minimum of **75% attendance** in each subject to be eligible to appear in the university end-semester examination. This is per subject, not an average across subjects.

If you fall below 75% in even one subject, you are technically **not eligible (detained)** for the exam in that subject.

## How Attendance Is Counted

Attendance is counted per lecture, practical, and tutorial session. Most colleges track it subject-by-subject. A full semester typically runs 13–15 weeks, giving roughly **45–60 lectures per subject**.

75% of 60 lectures = you must attend at least **45 lectures** in that subject.

## What Is Condonation?

Condonation is an official relaxation SPPU offers for students who fall between **65% and 74%** attendance in a subject. If you're in this range, you can apply for condonation — your college forwards the request to SPPU with a reason (medical, family emergency, etc.), and if approved, you're allowed to sit the exam.

Condonation is **not automatic**. The college must verify the reason, and the final call is SPPU's. It's also not guaranteed.

> Students below 65% are generally not eligible for condonation and are detained in that subject.

## Detained vs Eligible

| Attendance    | Status                                |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 75% and above | Eligible to sit exam                  |
| 65% – 74%     | May apply for condonation             |
| Below 65%     | Detained — cannot sit exam regardless |

## Consequences of Being Detained

If you're detained in a subject:

1. You **cannot appear** in the university exam for that subject this semester
2. The subject carries forward as an **F grade / KT (Keep Term)**
3. You must repeat the subject next time it's offered — often the same semester next year
4. This delays your graduation if it happens in a critical subject

## What Actually Happens in Practice

Most colleges issue an attendance warning midway through the semester. Pay attention to these warnings — it's much easier to fix your attendance in weeks 8–10 than to chase condonation in week 14.

If you know you'll be travelling, hospitalised, or unable to attend for a stretch, inform your department in writing with documentation. This creates a paper trail that supports any condonation request later.

## How to Track Your Attendance

Don't rely on informal counts. Most colleges now have an online portal (or the college ERP) where your attendance is updated weekly. Check it at least once every two weeks.

A quick formula to check if you're on track:

> Minimum lectures to attend = 0.75 × Total lectures held so far

If you're below this number at any point, calculate how many remaining lectures you need to attend to reach 75% by the end of semester.
